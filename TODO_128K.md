- [ ] **More variance in where hostiles and asteroids appear, from the front 270 degrees (idea 2026-11-21; phase 1, the hostiles, done 2026-11-21):** **Done:** `SPAWN_VARIANCE` in `src/elite128/config.inc` (on; remove the define for the original rules): `spawnShip` ends with `call spawnVariance` (the 3 free glue bytes), the routine sits in the overlay on `bankInit` (`lasersights128.asm`, `bankInit + 0xB7`, 59 bytes) and changes only the hostile types (bit 2 of byte `+33`): x_hi and y_hi random 0 to 63, z_hi 32 to 63, z sign random, a ship behind the player inside the rear cone (x_hi and y_hi both below z_hi) goes to the front. The original design notes: today (all 8-bit versions, `docs/_ship_spawning.md`) a hostile starts at x, y, z of about 12,800 in one of four diagonal corners ahead, an asteroid within about ±2,300 of the centre line at z 12,800, and everything only ahead (the sign of z is never set). Wanted: a random distance and direction for both. **Hostiles:** from the front 270 degrees (everything except the back 90 degrees cone), flying towards the player. **Asteroids:** any position around the player (no back cone) and any heading (decided 2026-11-21: they drift, they are not aimed). Design thoughts:
  - **Position without trigonometry:** random high bytes for x, y and z (for example x_hi, y_hi random 0 to `&3F`, z_hi random `&20`-`&3F`, so the distance varies between about 8,000 and 16,000) and random signs for all three. The back cone is spared with one comparison: if z is negative and both x_hi and y_hi are smaller than z_hi, flip the z sign (or draw again). That leaves the front 180 degrees plus the two 45 degree side wedges behind the lateral axis = 270 degrees (as a square pyramid, close enough to a cone). Asteroids skip the test: random signs for x, y and z are enough.
  - **Heading:** hostiles aim at the player through their own tactics. Asteroids have no AI (`spawnCommand0` clears it) and keep the identity orientation, i.e. they all fly along +z; for a random heading give each one a random orientation: `setDirectionofEntity` at `&82B8` builds the matrix from a direction vector, so a random vector (the signed random bytes, no normalising needed if the routine copes) may be enough. Fully random headings would send many asteroids spawned at 12,800 units away off for good, so **bias the heading towards a point near the player** (decided 2026-11-21): aim at the player plus a random offset of a few thousand units in each axis, so most pass through or near the player's area and some hit (the ST points its asteroids at the player). Anything from the front 270 degrees or from behind, but aimed.
  - **Packs:** `spawnEscortPack` calls `spawnShip` per ship, so with variance the wingmen would scatter over the sky: spawn the leader with the variance and put the others at the leader's position plus a small random offset.
  - **Where the code goes** (solved for the hostiles): the glue has no byte left, so the rules live in the overlay on `bankInit` (6 bytes left of 248, the stage slot holds 256); an asteroid heading routine would need more room: a far-call module (bank 6 has about 2.3 KB of code space) or a second overlay region.
  - **Checks:** the sampling script of the pirates item (call `spawnShip` 80 times, histogram of the signs and of the distance), a test that no spawn lands in the back cone, screenshots in the front, side and rear views; the missions (Constrictor, Thargoid, the marked Asp, Trumbles) and the safe zone must behave as before; frame time with ships at the sides (the fill clamps to the viewport).
  - **Must be a compile-time switch** (`-DSPAWN_VARIANCE`; in the 128K build set or unset in `src/elite128/config.inc`, like the other flags): it is a deliberate difference from the original game, so with the flag off the spawn code and its bytes stay exactly as they are, and `src/elite` stays byte-exact (`check_src.sh`). Today (`todoDrawExplosions`, `common2x.asm`): a sound and a circular explosion, 15 expanding rings (`drawradius` + 2 per ring, `SUB_ram_e74c`) at the screen position cached for the exploding slot (`cache_explosion_screen_pos`, flag bits 4 and 5), with `sfxFlags2Bitmap` bit 6 for the sound. The C64 and BBC (`DOEXP`/`PTCLS` in `work/ext_c64`, `ext_bbc`): at each of the first n projected vertices (n from the blueprint's explosion-count byte) a cloud of 1 to 8 particles per vertex, offsets = random x cloud size, size = 8 x cloud counter / distance, the counter starting at 18 and rising by 4 per frame; the random numbers come from four seed bytes kept per ship, XORed with the vertex index, so the cloud is the same every frame (it expands coherently and the second pass can erase it by XOR); points are clipped to the screen and plotted with a size from the distance; the C64 adds a sprite flash on the first frame. How it fits here:
  - **Keep the sound and the ring flash** as the first frame (like the C64's sprite flash); the particles take over after it.
  - **No erase pass:** the off-screen bitmap is wiped every frame; the per-ship seed stays, so the cloud is coherent.
  - **Code in a bank 6 module, not in the glue** (no byte free): replace the ring code in bank 7 by a far-call stub (the `hullDraw` pattern: resident stack, config C6, call, `mapMain`, stack back) and put the particle code (about 150-250 bytes) in bank 6 (about 2.3 KB code room) with its own pixel plot (bank 7 is hidden in C6); it needs a new stage chunk in the blank bytes of `COMMON2X` (check blankness in `build_dsk128.py`) and a vector next to `fillHullEntry`. Per-slot state (cloud counter, 4 seed bytes, 8 slots) in a bank 6 table, reset when a slot starts to explode.
  - **Phase 1:** one cloud at the cached screen centre, radius from the counter and the distance. **Phase 2:** clouds at the vertices: copy the first n projected vertices (`hullVertexBuf` already holds them for the fill) into the slot's table when it starts to explode, cap n at about 12; check that the explosion-count byte of the CPC blueprint is where the 6502 header has it.
  - **Lifetime in time:** the counter step must be larger than the 6502's 4 (the CPC draws 5 to 6 frames per second), or an explosion that lasts about 60 frames there would last 10 seconds.
  - **Cost:** up to about 28 vertices x 8 particles = 220 dots a frame, roughly 10 ms; clip to the 256 x 128 viewport, skip vertices behind the viewer; the explosion is drawn right after the hull queue is flushed, so it is on top already.
