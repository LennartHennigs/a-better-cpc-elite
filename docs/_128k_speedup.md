# 128K speedup: measurements, what was changed, what is left

Written 2026-11-21 after the first fair frame-time comparison of the 64K and 128K builds. Summary table: `docs/_128k_version.md` "Cost". Open items: `TODO_128K.md`. Diary: `CHANGELOG_128K.md`.

## How to measure (and what went wrong before)

Tool: `tools/tests/test_frametime.py DISK SNA SYMFILE [options]`, run from `work/xray` with cpcxray's Python and an absolute script path (usage in its docstring; `tools/tests/README.md` row). It stops at `handleEntitys` (once per frame) and reports the emulated time between stops.

- `--phases` also stops at marks and prints the mean time from the previous stop to each one. Default marks: `transferBuffertoScreen` and, on the 128K build, the flip (`dbFlipTail`, then `dbfw1`, `dbfCmp`, `dbfGaps` looked up by name in the `fill.sym` that sits next to the sym file). `--marks A,B,...` (implies `--phases`) takes any symbol names (also from `fill.sym`) or hex addresses instead; names the symbol files lack are skipped, so one list serves both builds.
- Setup options: `--clean` calls `cleanShipSlots` first, `--poke ADDR=HEX,...` writes bytes, `--ship TYPE[,ZHIGH]` spawns a ship straight ahead, `--shot FILE.png`.
- Symbols: 128K `work/elite128/c128.sym` (regenerated by `check_src128.sh`), 64K `work/elite128/c.sym` (stock addresses; `elite64.dsk` has the same ones for everything measured here).
- Snapshots: 128K `output/flight128.sna` (`test_flight128.py 3`; **regenerate after every build**: the fill module and the glue are inside it, a stale one runs the old code); 64K stock `output/firebird_in_flight.sna`; `elite64.dsk` needs one made by booting it (the scratch copy of `test_flight128.py` with `RUN"ELITE64`, a 60 s boot wait and `output/flight64e.sna`; not kept in the repo).

Pitfalls found on the way:

1. **The old script's addresses were stale** (`lookforFreeSlot` `&816E`, `setDirectionofEntity` `&82BB` in the 128K build, the script called `&816B` and `&82B8`, mid-instruction). The "232 vs 254 ms" figures in the TODO and in `_128k_version.md` were not reproducible; the 128K number came out at 356 ms with the stale calls.
2. **The two launch snapshots are different scenes.** The 128K one (a new commander just launched) has a second occupied slot beside the planet; the 64K `firebird_in_flight.sna` has the planet only. Use `--clean`.
3. **The planet is inside scanner range after a launch.** `UpdateScanner` loops over slot 0 as well and sets `todo_CleanScanCounter` as soon as a slot passes the coarse range test (high bytes below `&40`), before it knows whether a blip lands inside the ellipse. So the scanner counts as busy every frame while the ship stays near the planet. This is the stock logic, not a 128K bug; the 64K game pays for it too (24 ms of `UpdateScanner` work). `--poke 1A47=50` (slot 0, z high byte) moves the planet out of range and the busy state ends.
4. **A dead player gives identical numbers whatever you poke.** An earlier `flight128.sna` was in "Game Over"; a planet placed at z below its radius also kills the player. Take a screenshot (`--shot`) before trusting a run.
5. The first frames after `--ship` are very slow (a close ship fills a large area): compare the fastest frames, or the mean of 16+ frames, not the first few.

## Findings (launch scene, planet in view and in scanner range, slots cleared)

Frame: 64K stock 201 ms, `elite64.dsk` 202 ms, 128K 276 ms before the change, 240 ms after. Phases (ms; 64K = `elite64.dsk`):

| Phase | 64K | 128K before | 128K after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `handleEntitys` to `handleLaser` (entity loop, only the planet in it) | 7.6 | 14.7 | 14.7 |
| `handleLaser` (laser sights) | 3.4 | 10.5 | 10.5 |
| `transferBuffertoScreen` | 54.4 | 55.1 | 55.1 |
| flip: wait for the vertical sync | | 6 to 13 | 6 to 8 |
| flip: cockpit copy | | 47.1 | 17.9 |
| `UpdateIndicatorBars` | 24.5 | 27.1 | 27.1 |
| dust, planet, dashboard, scanner draw, rest | | | within 1 ms per routine |

- The flip was 79% of the 128K overhead (58 of 75 ms). Its path in `dbFlip6` (`fill.asm`): vsync wait, CRTC switch, change detection, then ONE of: everything (8 x 512 bytes, when a gauge variable changed and every 64th frame), the scanner zone (56 strips of 32 bytes) while `dbScanFlag`, or the compass (24 strips of 6 bytes), then three small copies. The scanner zone is the expensive one.
- Why the old copy took 47 ms and not the 11 ms of `ldir` work: it kept interrupts off and was paced (160 bytes per interrupt, then `halt`). A chunk that outlasts the 3.3 ms gap between interrupts is held until the next `ei`, and the next `halt` then waits one more full period: a window of 384 bytes (interrupts still off) gave only 34 ms and 172 us jitter because each window cost two periods. The largest window that always fits is about 2.3 ms of copying; this was not pursued once the interrupt-enabled copy worked.
- Sampling (`pcsample` style: `run` a few hundred microseconds, read PC, bucket by nearest label) is of little use here: `c128.sym` has far fewer labels in the moved code than `c.sym`, so the same routine lands in different buckets. Marks at routine entries are reliable.

## Changed (2026-11-21)

`dbCopy` (`commonxx.asm`) has no `di`/`ei` (two `nop`s keep every later address); the pacing code (`dbCopyB`, `DBWIN`, `dbBudget` in `fill.asm`) is deleted, `dbFlip6` calls `dbCopy` directly and enables interrupts once at the start. (An intermediate build kept `dbCopyB` with `DBWIN` = `0x7FFF` and one `halt` per flip; `/simplify` removed that dead bookkeeping, about 94 T-states per copy.) Why it is safe: the interrupt handlers (`myVBL` `&2815`, `IRSound`, `IRKeyboardInput` `&8C94`, `dbIsr` `&9D2E`, the music hook in the `bankInit` overlay) all sit below `&A000` (banks 0 and 2), which every flip configuration maps (C0, C1/C3, C6), and none reads or writes `&4000-&7FFF` or `&C000+`. `ldir` is interruptible every iteration (about 5 us). The title music hook (view 0 flips too) treats a PC outside `&4000-&7FFF` with SP below `&C000` as an unknown configuration and skips its tick, as it did while interrupts were off.

Result: scanner copy 47 -> 18 ms, frame 276 -> 240 ms, worst interrupt entry lateness (`test_isrjitter128.py`, 600 entries, launch scene with the station) 221 -> 5 us (stock 5 us), `test_flip128.py` 0 of 4096 cockpit bytes differ at the end, `run_all.py --emu128` 7 of 7, 10-minute soak (`test_soak128.py 10`) without a reset, the disk rebuilt after the comment edits is byte-identical to the tested one.

If a glitch ever shows up in the raster split or the title music, the old behaviour needs the two `nop`s back to `di`/`ei` and the paced `dbCopyB` from git history (before 2026-11-21, `DBWIN` 160).

## What is left, in the order I would try it

1. **The vertical-sync wait (6 to 13 ms), and why frame times come in steps.** The flip waits for a vsync, so the 128K frame is quantised to whole video frames: 239.6 ms is 12 x 19.97 ms, 259.8 ms is 13, 200.1 ms is 10 (the 64K game has no wait and is continuous). Work saved elsewhere only shows once it crosses a step: the simplified copy (17.9 ms instead of 22.2 after dropping `dbCopyB` and the `halt`) left the frame at 239.6 ms because the wait grew from 6.2 to 12.5 ms. Removing the wait would turn that slack into speed (about 10 ms on average) and make every later saving count at once. `dbfw1`/`dbfw2` wait for the next vsync before writing R12. The CRTC takes a new start address only at the start of the next frame, so the write may not need the wait: the view area of the new draw bank is not touched until the next transfer (about 200 ms later), the cockpit copies and the direct cockpit drawing only change the old bank early, which is the same thing the stock game does on its live screen. Untested; check on a real CPC or at least with Virtual Retro that nothing tears (cpcxray cannot show a CRTC race), keep the `di` around the R12 write and the first-copy `halt`.
2. **Scanner copy, still 18 ms.** About 11 ms of `ldir` (1,792 bytes), the rest is per-strip overhead (`dbZone` loop, `dbCopy`: 56 calls, each with a register-pair mapping) and the interrupt handlers. Options: a dirty rectangle (`tools/cockpit_rects.py`, planned and not written: the scanner ellipse is about 29 of the 32 columns, so a box from the projected blip positions is the only real saving), copy whole 448-byte row runs where the zone is nearly full width (more bytes, fewer calls: not a gain by itself), unrolled `ldi` (16 instead of 21 T-states per byte).
3. **Mark the scanner busy only when a blip is drawn.** `todo_CleanScanCounter` is set before the projection checks in `UpdateScanner` (`common2x.asm`, `ld a,1 / ld (todo_CleanScanCounter),a`). Moving those 5 bytes to `uscn4` (after the blip passed the ellipse test, net size 0) would end the busy state for a planet that is in range but outside the ellipse. This changes stock behaviour (cleaning is skipped when nothing was drawn, which is also correct) and shifts labels inside `common2x.asm`: run `sync_equs.py --write` and `check_moved_labels.py`. Not tried; check first whether the planet blip is drawn at all in the scene (the scanner showed a dot at the launch).
4. **The entity loop, +7 ms with only the planet in it (14.7 against 7.6 ms).** Not traced. Candidates: the spawn parser on its countdown frames (`spawnVariance` uses `approx_sqrt_16bit` and `mult_b_c_msb`, which dominated the PC samples of that window), `hullBegin`/`hullFlush`. Put marks inside (`--marks spawnCommandParser,hullFlush,...`) and look at the frames where the spawn counter fires.
5. **The laser sights, 10.5 ms against 3.4 ms for the stock crosshair.** The overlay on `bankInit` (`lasersights128.asm`, `laserSightsHome`) draws more than four lines; check whether it can reuse the stock line routine or draw fewer pixels.
6. **`UpdateIndicatorBars`, +2.6 ms.** The 128K screen layout makes some bar pixels more expensive; low priority.
7. **Not worth doing:** direct drawing into the back screen (the plan's phase 4). The transfer from the bitmap to the screen costs the same 55 ms in both builds, so the page flip did not add a copy of the view; removing the transfer would be a new speedup for both builds, not a fix of a 128K cost.

Other numbers for orientation: a Cobra straight ahead (type 12, z `&100`) takes 252 ms at its fastest frame on the 128K build (279 ms before the change) against 201 ms on the 64K stock one; the 128K fill itself is not the bottleneck in these scenes.
