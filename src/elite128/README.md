## Projected vertex format (read off the stock engine, `tools/probe_vertices.py`, title-screen Cobra)

`buf_5860` (`&5860`), 3 bytes per vertex: x magnitude, y magnitude, flag. Flag bit 0 = y below the centre, bit 1 = x left of the centre, bit 7 = off view.
`CalcPointAdressinBitmap` (`&7EFC`) shows the pixel position: x_pix = 127 - xmag if flag bit 1 else 127 + xmag; y_pix = 63 + ymag if flag bit 0 else 63 - ymag
(256 x 128 bitmap, centre (127, 63), 64 bytes per line from `&A000`). The line routine `SUB_ram_7c45` gets the same magnitudes in HL/DE plus the sign bits in C.
So the fill module takes (x mag, y mag, flag) and computes absolute x/y itself; vertices with bit 7 set are skipped (clip v1).
Blueprint header (23 bytes): +3 vertices, +4 edges, +5 faces, +11 LOD distance, +12/+14/+16 offsets of vertices/edges/faces, +19 zoom (`parseFaces` stores it).
`todo_tableforedges` (16 bytes, initial 0x01) is the face-visible table; with 0 faces `parseFaces` writes entry 0 = 1, and a vertex whose face nibbles are 0 is always computed,
which is what `tools/ship128.py` relies on.

## Page flip (2026-11-11)

Flight views are double buffered: `transferBuffertoScreen` ends with `jp dbFlipTail` (resident, `commonxx.asm`), which maps RAM bank 6 (config C6, resident stack like `hullDraw`) and calls `dbFlip6` (`fill.asm` at `&4400`, assembled with the resident addresses by `tools/check_src128.sh`). `dbFlip6`: waits for the vertical sync, writes R12 (`&1C` bank 1, `&3C` bank 3) for the bank just drawn, makes the other bank the draw bank (`curCfg` C1 <-> C3), then copies from the shown bank to the new draw bank the cockpit rows 16-23 of every scan line block (8 x 512 bytes) and the two 16-byte gap variable areas (`+0x0E00`, `+0x2FF0`), in 256-byte chunks through `dbCopy` (resident, C0, interrupts off per chunk). The first flight frame after a non-flight view copies the whole 16 KB page. Non-flight views (`Viewdirection >= 4`) draw into the displayed bank: `dbIsr`, called from `IRSound` in place of the dead music test, points R12 at the draw bank and clears `dbOn`.
Free resident space was the limit (the bank 7 block has 0 bytes spare, the real page gaps hold the font), hence bank 6 for the flip body. `tools/tests/test_flip128.py`: curCfg alternates every frame, the two cockpits are equal, a 3 minute soak ran without a reset. Cost: about 21 ms of copying per frame plus the vsync wait (flight frame rate about 4.5 per second with a planet filling the view, was 6).
Cockpit sync by zones (commit after 5d3c33f): `dbFlipTail` computes whether the scanner is busy (`todo_CleanScanCounter` or `ecmActiveFlag` set, plus one frame for the clean-up) and passes it as `dbScanFlag`; `dbFlip6` then copies (a) everything (8 x 512 bytes) when one of the 29 player variables the bars are drawn from (`movementspeed` .. `IndicatorPitch`, they include the bars' "last drawn" copies) or `missileCount` differs from the snapshot in bank 6, and in any case every 8th frame (alert light, station and ECM letters have no variable of their own here); else (b) the scanner zone (columns 16-47 of rows 17-23, 56 strips of 32 bytes) if `dbScanFlag`, else (c) the compass (columns 42-47 of rows 17-19). The two 16-byte gap variable areas are copied every frame. No hooks in the drawing routines were needed (the bank 7 block has no spare bytes). Cost per frame: the old 21 ms only when a ship is in scanner range (the scanner zone is about as expensive as the whole cockpit because of the per-strip call overhead and the interrupts), otherwise the compass strips only, about 3 ms. `getPrevScreenLineAdr` (compass dot) had a `cp 0xC0` for the old page and is fixed.
Tunnels (launch, hyperspace, docking), explosions, laser lines, flight messages and the death sequence draw into the bitmap and go through `transferBuffertoScreen`, so they flip like everything else (checked: launch tunnel frames, a hyperspace jump, a forced explosion with its bounty message).
Not done: save/load under C1/C3, docking from flight, the chart keys in cpcxray, a finer scanner rectangle (a ship-position based bounding box would cut the scanner-busy cost).

Interrupt pacing of the flip copy (2026-11-12, superseded 2026-11-21 by the next paragraph): the copy kept interrupts off per chunk and was paced by `halt` at 160 bytes per interrupt, which kept the raster-timed colour split within 4 us (p95) and 215 us (worst) at a frame rate with the scanner busy of about 3.8 per second.

Copy with interrupts enabled (2026-11-21): `dbCopy` has no `di`/`ei` (two `nop`s keep every address), the pacing (`dbCopyB`, `DBWIN`, `dbBudget`) is deleted and `dbFlip6` calls `dbCopy` directly. Safe because the interrupt handlers sit below `&A000` in banks 0 and 2, which every configuration of the flip maps, and use nothing in `&4000-&7FFF` or `&C000+` (reasoning at `dbCopy` in `commonxx.asm`). Measured: scanner zone copy 47 ms -> 18 ms per frame, interrupt entry jitter worst 5 us (was 221 us; stock 5 us), `test_flip128.py` 0 of 4096 cockpit bytes differ, `run_all.py --emu128` 7 of 7. Numbers: `docs/_128k_version.md` "Cost"; method and next steps: `docs/_128k_speedup.md`.

Indicator events (2026-11-12): the gauge snapshot compare of `dbFlip6` now also watches `missileCount`, `missileLockState` and `FlagStationNear` (table `dbWatch`; ECM already keeps the scanner zone busy through `dbFlipTail`), so the alert/missile/station/ECM items reach the other bank on the frame they change; the safety net is every 64th frame instead of every 8th. `tools/tests/test_flip128.py` presses the indicator keys and checks that both cockpits are equal after every flip; forcing `ecmActiveFlag` and `FlagStationNear` by poking memory also keeps them equal (the keys alone do not fit an ECM or missiles on a fresh commander, so the poke run is the real check).

Clipping (2026-11-12): the flag byte 0xFF in `buf_5860` means the vertex is behind the viewer (the carry comes from the sign of the depth coordinate), not that it is far outside the view: far-out vertices keep their (clamped, 8 bit) magnitudes and `getVertex` clamps them to the view, so hulls that leave the view on a side are drawn cut at the border (checked with `POS=0x90,0,0x180 test_hulls128.py 12` and five more positions). Only hulls with a vertex behind the viewer lose the triangles at that vertex, as the stock wireframe loses the edges; a near plane clip would need the depth, which is not passed to the fill. Fixed on the way: a vertex 193 or more below the centre wrapped to the top of the view.

Depth key (2026-11-12): the deferred hull list sorts on the 16 bit depth (high byte, then low byte of the position along the view axis); queue entries are 8 bytes (`hullQ` 64 bytes at `&8000`). Checked by reading the order of the `hullDraw` calls for two ships whose depths share the high byte (`ZS=0x210,0x2F0 test_order128.py 11 12`, and the reverse slot order): the far ship is always drawn first.

One variant (2026-11-12): the 128K build always has the missions, the enhanced UI and the title ship selection; `src/elite128/config.inc` holds the `DEFINE`s, so a plain `sjasmplus file.asm` gives the same bytes as the build (the `--missions` disk was the only tested one; the former default disk had glue labels computed with the missions flag and did not boot into the entity loop). `build_dsk128.py` writes `build/elite128.dsk` (`--missions` is ignored). `tools/check_moved_labels.py` lists raw numbers that still equal a stock address whose label moved (it found nothing after the four stale digram and clear addresses were replaced by labels).

## Title screens: text cache (2026-11-13)

The title screens are double buffered like flight, because single buffering showed the cleared bitmap while the ship was drawn and the text flickered. The heading (text row 1) and prompt (row 14) were printed into the bitmap every frame (about 65 ms of a 320 ms frame). After one printed frame `ttHook` (`fill_tt.asm`, called by `dbFlip6` before the flip) patches the first byte of `printSplashscreen1` and `printPressSpace` to `ret`; every later frame it copies columns 8-59 of those 2 x 8 lines from the screen on show into the draw screen. The text is on both screens all the time. The cache key is `splash2Shipnr` without the key-down bit 7; the press-space screen sets bit 6 (`titleNext`) so it differs from the first screen; a changed key or any frame that is not a title frame (`getTitleKey` sets `titleSeen`, `ttHook` clears it) unpatches the printers (that one frame has no text). A ship reaching the first or last text row would show the previous frame's pixels there.
Title keys: the title frames take about 250 ms, so `dbIsr` latches the last key seen on the title (`titleKeyIsr`, status 0) and the title loops read it with `getTitleKey` (in the padding behind `TITLE_NEXT`).
`fill.bin` must stay within 0x600 bytes (it rides in blank screen pixels of the window page): `dbFlip6Entry` is `&43D0` (just after the fill code) and `dbSnap` lives in the variable page `&4F40`.

Clipping to the frame (2026-11-13): `getVertex` clamps the projected vertex to the viewport interior (`VIEW_X0..VIEW_X1` = 9..246, `VIEW_Y0..VIEW_Y1` = 1..126; the frame is drawn into the bitmap at pixel columns 8 and 247 and rows 0 and 127), so a fill never paints over the frame or outside the view. `dbFlip6Entry` moved from `&43B8` to `&43D0` to pay for it. Still open (clip v2): a vertex more than 127 pixels off the centre is clamped, which bends the hull's outline (the engine hands over 8 bit magnitudes, exact up to 255), and triangles with a vertex behind the viewer are dropped. A real clipper (Sutherland-Hodgman on 16 bit coordinates, edge intersections by bisection) needs about 250 bytes in bank 6 plus a third stage slot and about 12 bytes in `bankInit`, where the resident glue had 3 bytes left (21 after tidying the disc trampolines and the draw queue entry address on 2026-11-13).

## Hull sources (2026-11-20)

The filled hulls come from one of two sources, chosen with `HULLS` (`tools/ship128.py --source stock|arcelite`; `make disk6128`): `stock` (the default) builds the triangles from the CPC's own blueprints with `tools/hullfill.py`, `arcelite` converts ArcElite's shapes as before (needs `work/arcelite/`). The stock source takes each face's outline from the edges that name it, cuts it into triangles wound against the stock face normal, draws further loops inside a face (exhaust ports, cockpit lines) as detail triangles in the accent colour, and bakes the shading from a key light (upper front left) and a fill light into five dither patterns (`RAMP`: dim hull colour, hull colour with the accent, solid hull colour; the accent is kept for details; a hull whose faces differ very little, the Thargoid, is stretched to use the whole ramp). `design()` adds a few details of our own: the Cobra's windscreen triangle, the Sidewinder's canopy and wing trim, the Viper's canopy. The Constrictor is based on Fessor's hull: his header and vertices, triangles from his edges and faces. Both sources fill the same fixed slot (`HULL_REGION_SIZE`, 1,781 bytes, padded), so the layout is identical and `sync_equs.py` is not needed when switching; the ArcElite build is byte-identical to the one before the switch was added. ## Title music (2026-11-21)

The Blue Danube plays on the title screens only. `musiquex.asm` is the stock player assembled for the slot it had in the 64K game (`&6000-&6FFF`, 4096 bytes of flight-only code that the music replaced while the title and docked screens ran); in this build that slot is `&DF02-&EF01` of the code block in RAM bank 7 (`loc_604A` is its `+&4A`, `MUSIC_BASE = loc_604A - 0x4A`, generated `equ`). `bankInit` loads `MUSIQUEX.BIN` over it at boot, `loadNotMusique` reads `NOTMUSIC.BIN` back (`tools/build_dsk128.py` writes the 4096 bytes of `COMMON2X` there). The interrupt calls the tick from `dbIsr` (title branch) through `musicHook`, which rides in the overlay on `bankInit` (`lasersights128.asm`, `bankInit + 0x80`, `musicState`: 0 off, 1 on, n > 1 owes n - 1 ticks): as in the stock `IRSound` the music replaces the sound effects. Bank 7 is only visible in the draw configurations, so the hook looks at the interrupted SP (`stack5`, myVBL runs the ISR on its own stack): below `&C000` means a far call is active (the ship fill), the tick is owed and `MusicRun` runs up to 24 of them later. The player never calls the firmware (its stock start and stop routines did) and its PSG writes no longer end in `ei`. The order in `loadNotMusique` matters: `musicState` = 0 and `mute` first, then `loadLARGEAXX` (it puts the parked firmware RAM back, which the disc routines need), then the disc read. Test: `tools/tests/test_music128.py`.

## Spawn variance (2026-11-21)

Compile switch `SPAWN_VARIANCE` (`config.inc`). `spawnShip` (resident glue) ends with `call spawnVariance`; the routine is in the overlay on `bankInit` (`lasersights128.asm`, `bankInit + 0xB7`, after the music hook) and changes only the hostile types (blueprint byte `+33`, bit 2): x_hi and y_hi random 0 to 63, z_hi 32 to 63, z sign random, a ship behind the player with x_hi and y_hi both below z_hi is flipped to the front, so ships come from the front 270 degrees. The x and y signs and the low bytes are still the stock random ones. Since the second phase (2026-11-21) the rules live in a module in RAM bank 6 (`spawnmod.asm`, `&4600`, 564 bytes: code and the 27-row orientation table `spawn_table.inc` from `tools/mk_spawntable.py`); the overlay holds only the stubs `spawnVariance` (`bankInit + 0xB7`), `spawnRock` (`+0xBC`, jumped to by the asteroid branch of `spawnCommand0`) and `spawnPackShip` (`+0xDA`, called by `spawnEscortPack` for every pack member), which switch to the resident stack and config C6 like the fill module. The module places an asteroid anywhere around the player and gives it the nose direction towards the player (plus a random offset, cut to one of 26 directions); the members of a pack copy the leader's position with a small shake. `bankInit` copies the module from the window-page block at `STAGE+0x3000`. Details and the comparison with the other versions: `docs/_ship_spawning.md`; test: `tools/tests/test_spawnvar128.py`.
